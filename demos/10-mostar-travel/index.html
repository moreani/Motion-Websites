Chapter 00 — Arrival

Mostar, at the pace of the river.

A city of one bridge, two banks and an afternoon that never quite arrives; come for the crossing, stay for the coffee.

Stari Most · Neretva · 43°20′N 17°49′E

01 — The bridge

Six centuries, one arch.

Stari Most was rebuilt stone by stone in 2004, with the same limestone, the same curve and the same twenty-nine metre span.

02 — The current

The Neretva runs cold, even in August.

Snowmelt from the Prenj mountains holds the river near twelve degrees, which is why the divers wait so long on the parapet.

Field notes · No. 04

Three hours, one bridge, no plan.

Mostar rewards the unhurried. Start on the east bank before the coaches arrive, cross once, and let the lanes do the rest.

  1. i

    Kujundžiluk, the coppersmiths' lane

    A single crooked street of copper trays, coffee pots and small workshops. Walk it slowly; the best doors are half open.

    East bank
    07:00 — 21:00

  2. ii

    Koski Mehmed Pasha Mosque

    Climb the minaret for the only view that shows the arch from above. The courtyard stays cool through the middle of the day.

    West bank
    15 min climb

  3. iii

    Coffee on the east parapet

    Order a small bosanska kafa and sit facing the water. The river does most of the talking between six and eight.

    Riverside
    18:00 onwards

“Every city has a centre. Mostar has a span — and everyone crosses it twice: once to arrive, once to understand.”

Field note 04 — evening

East bank · 06:40
Before the light

When to come April to June, or late September

Before the August crowds. Mornings are cool, the light is soft, and the bridge is almost yours.

How to arrive Train from Sarajevo, two hours

The line follows the Neretva valley most of the way. Sit on the left; the station sits above the old town.

Where to sleep One night on the east bank

Small rooms above the bazaar. Ask for a window facing the bridge, then leave it open.
